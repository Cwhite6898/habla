The $21,000 line, explained

One item in the underspend table above — "Prepaid expense adjustment," +$21,000 — isn't money we chose not to spend. It's an accounting entry for a science program that was paid for in June 2025 (last fiscal year) but delivered this year. Depending on which fiscal year it's ultimately booked to:

09Looking ahead to 2026–27

Our finance policy defines a specific way to answer "how much of this money is actually uncommitted?" Rather than treating the whole bank balance as available, it holds back a full year of planned expenses plus a cushion in case fundraising underperforms.

The formula

Excess Funds  =  June Cash Balance  −  Next Year's Projected Expenses  −  a 5–10% buffer against Next Year's Projected Revenue

StepAmount
Cash in the bank, June 30, 2026$256,418.81

Depending on where the board sets the buffer, roughly $115,000 to $121,000 is uncommitted heading into the new school year. Under the policy, money in this category is directed toward non-recurring uses — one-time expenses or multi-year special projects — approved by the board at the start of the school year. It isn't intended to fund ongoing operating costs, since those have to be sustainable on each year's fundraising.

10About these numbers

The $21,000 science program — under review

In June 2025 (the prior fiscal year), HABLA paid $21,000 for the Schmahl Science program, which ran during this fiscal year. That payment correctly created a prepaid asset on the balance sheet at the time.

When the program ran this year, a journal entry recognized the $21,000 as this year's Science Workshops expense — that part is right, and it's why the Science Workshops line correctly shows $21,048. But the entry credited that $21,000 back out through an expense account rather than clearing the original prepaid asset. The practical effect: the $21,000 expense gets cancelled out of this year's total instead of properly reducing the leftover asset, and — as of the June 30, 2026 balance sheet — the $21,000 asset is still sitting on the books for a program that's already finished.

We're working with our bookkeeper on the correcting entry and confirming which fiscal year it should land in. Until that's settled, this report shows figures both ways rather than picking one — see the flag at the top of this report and the range shown in "How we came out."

  • Where these figures come from. QuickBooks Statement of Activity Detail, Budget vs. Actuals, and Statement of Financial Position for July 2025–June 2026, the June 30, 2026 Wells Fargo statements, and transaction exports from our online fundraising platform.
  • A correction from an earlier draft. A $392.98 interest payment posted to savings on June 30, 2026 was initially missing from the revenue total in this report. It's since been confirmed in an updated QuickBooks export and is now included — Total Revenue and Net Income both reflect it.
  • Cash basis. Income counts when the money arrives, expenses when they're paid — not when they're pledged or invoiced.
  • What our fundraising platform costs us. $1,698 in fees on $71,933 charged to donors, a 2.36% average, across the February–June window covered by the payout report. By event: Gala $781 (2.45%), Caminata $609 (2.20%), Yearbook $200 (2.55%), Buy-a-Spot $100 (2.33%).
  • Why item counts don't always match the bank exactly. The platform reports what each family was charged, then pays out in batches a few days later. Item tables show what sold; the bank statement is the record of cash received. The difference is the fee above plus normal timing lag.
  • "Restricted" funds. Food Drive donations were given for that purpose specifically and can only be spent on it.
